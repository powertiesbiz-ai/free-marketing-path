# Free marketing teardowns

A teardown is a public, permission-based walk through one real business's marketing funnel. It is Step 5's diagnostic habit, Step 11's measurement discipline, and Step 10's barter logic combined: the volunteer gets useful outside eyes; the community gets a concrete example instead of abstract advice.

## How it works

1. A business owner volunteers through the community channels once they are live.
2. The owner gives **written permission** before anything is examined for publication.
3. We walk the public funnel the way a prospective customer would — from search or social profile to website, lead magnet, booking, and follow-up.
4. The volunteer receives the written teardown.
5. An agreed version is published in this repo and discussed in the community. The business is named only with the volunteer's consent.

No teardown is a sales ambush. If the work exposes a problem the volunteer wants help fixing, that is a separate conversation.

## What we examine

- **The offer** — Is it specific? Is the next step obvious? Does the price or entry point make sense where it appears?
- **The website** — Does the home page answer what, who, where, and why trust you — quickly, on a phone?
- **Google presence** — Business Profile completeness, categories, photos, reviews, review freshness, Search Console/indexing signals visible from the public site, and consistency of business facts.
- **Content** — Is anything being published on a sustainable rhythm? Does it answer buyer questions or merely announce the business?
- **The funnel to booking** — Is there a free diagnostic or lead magnet? Where does a visitor go after it? Can they book and pay without email ping-pong?
- **Follow-up and measurement** — Is there a CRM or another reliable system? Are leads answered, tracked, graded, and learned from?
- **Partnership and community surface** — Are there obvious referral, barter, or community opportunities the business is not using?

We only report what can be supported from the volunteer's information or the public funnel. Internal analytics, revenue, customer lists, and private systems are not required and should not be handed over for a public teardown.

## What the volunteer receives

- A written teardown using the fixed template below.
- The three highest-value fixes first, with free or near-free options named.
- A short “do not do yet” list, so a small problem does not become an expensive redesign.
- The right to correct factual errors before publication.
- A copy they can keep whether or not they want the public version published.

## What gets published, and where

With consent, the teardown may be published:

- In this repo, as a dated document under `docs/teardowns/` if the program grows into individual case files.
- In the powertiesbiz community channels, as a discussion post.
- In excerpts on social channels, linking back to the full teardown.

The business, owner, website, screenshots, and exact figures are included **only to the extent the volunteer consents in writing**. A teardown can be anonymized by industry and business size if that is the volunteer's condition. Private messages and unpublished business data never become content.

## How to volunteer

Volunteer through the community channels once they are live. Bring:

- Your public website and main profile links.
- The offer you most want customers to buy.
- One sentence about where you think the funnel is leaking.
- Written permission stating whether your business may be named.

Selection is not first-come at any cost. We will choose cases that teach different lessons — a missing Google presence, a good site with no lead magnet, a strong offer with no follow-up — so the series is useful to more than the volunteer.

## The barter logic

This is a fair trade, not a disguised giveaway:

- **The volunteer gets** a mini-diagnostic: a structured outside review they can act on without buying anything.
- **PowerTies gets** discussion content, proof of process, and a real example to teach from.
- **The community gets** a concrete case, including the trade-offs and numbers when the volunteer allows them.

That is win-win-win when consent is real and the teardown is useful even if the volunteer never becomes a customer.
