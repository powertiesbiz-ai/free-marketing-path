# Community launch plan — powertiesbiz

The community is Step 9 of [the path](the-path.md), done in public. We show up where business owners already talk, learn what they're actually doing to market themselves, share our own process — real numbers included — and point people to the free path when it's genuinely useful to them.

## Brand

**powertiesbiz**, everywhere:

- **Reddit** — u/powertiesbiz: participate in small-business and owner communities (e.g., r/smallbusiness and trade/owner subs). Participation first; the account is a person with a business, not a billboard.
- **Discord** — our own powertiesbiz server: home base for owners walking the path, sharing wins, failures, and numbers.
- **Nextdoor & Facebook groups** — participation, not brand pages. Keith joins as a local owner and neighbor; we don't spin up "brand presences" there.

## Goals

1. **Learn** what other owners are actually doing to market themselves for free — and steal (with credit) what works.
2. **Share our process openly** — the stack, the numbers, the failures. Openness is the marketing.
3. **Drive people to the repo and the path** — when it's relevant to the conversation, never as a drive-by.

## Rules of engagement

- **Disclose who we are.** Keith Tully, one-person consulting business (PowerTies), builder of the free path. No pretending to be a disinterested third party, ever.
- **Answer first.** Be useful in threads that have nothing to do with us. Self-mention comes last, and rarely.
- **No spam.** No copy-paste blasts, no link-dropping into unrelated threads, no DM pitches.
- **Platform rules respected.** Each subreddit and server sets its own self-promotion rules; we read them and stay inside them. Some communities ban links outright — that is fine, we participate anyway.
- **Every post is human-approved before publishing.** Nothing goes out under this brand that Keith hasn't read and approved. (See Bots, below — this rule is structural, not aspirational.)

## First three posts

### (a) The introduction — Reddit (r/smallbusiness) + Discord #introductions

**Title:** Here's the $0 marketing stack our one-person consulting business actually runs

> I'm Keith — I run a one-person consulting business (PowerTies) out of Queens, NY. Like most solo owners, my marketing budget is basically zero, so over the past year I built a system out of free tools. Sharing it in case it's useful, and because I want to hear what the rest of you are doing.
>
> The whole stack: a static website on free hosting (Cloudflare Pages) with a lead-capture form; Google Business Profile + Search Console so we get found; a homepage FAQ written the way people ask AI tools, with FAQ schema so ChatGPT/Gemini can quote us; two scheduled posts a week via Buffer's free tier; a free diagnostic quiz (we call it MLOT) that prescribes our one paid offer; Square payment links + online booking so nobody emails back and forth; Zoho CRM + n8n automation so every reply and lead lands in the CRM — with a hard rule that a human approves anything before it sends; a review ask after every job plus the free directory listings (Bing, Apple, Yelp, BBB, Nextdoor); showing up in communities like this one; referral partnerships and barter with businesses that serve the same customers; and a small daily outreach habit — about 10 verified emails a day, graded weekly.
>
> Total recurring cost: $13.49/mo for the little server that runs the automation. That's it — everything else is free tiers and elbow grease.
>
> I wrote the whole thing up as a free, open step-by-step path (link in comments / my profile — it's a GitHub repo called free-marketing-path, MIT licensed, take whatever's useful).
>
> What am I missing? What's in your free stack?

### (b) The question — Reddit + Discord #general

**Title:** Owners: what free tactic actually brought you a customer — not followers, a customer?

> Genuine research question. Everybody's feed is full of "post more content," but I want the specific thing that put money in the register.
>
> Ours, honestly: asking for a Google review after every finished job, with a direct link. Boring, free, and it compounds. Second place: a free diagnostic quiz on our site that shows owners where they're losing money — people book the paid session straight from their own results.
>
> Yours doesn't have to be digital. A barter deal, a partnership, a chalkboard sign — what actually worked? I'll compile the answers (with credit) into the open guide we're building.

### (c) The process post — Reddit + Discord #process

**Title:** Our outreach rule: grade it every week, and if replies are still zero at day 30, we fix the system — we don't just send more

> We do small daily outreach: about 10 emails a day to scored, verified leads. The part that changed everything wasn't the sending, it was the grading. Every Sunday we join the week's sends to the outcomes and look at the funnel — sent → delivered → replied → paid — broken down by how well each lead scored for fit.
>
> Two things grading caught that vibes never would have: (1) guessed email addresses were our bounce factory — nearly half our bounces early on. We removed guessing entirely and verify every address before it sends. (2) It's very possible to work hard for 30 days at a message nobody wants. So we set the checkpoint in advance: if genuine replies are still zero at day 30, we stop and diagnose properly — deliverability, message, targeting, offer, channel — instead of doubling the volume on a broken thing.
>
> We publish our numbers as this runs (they're humbling so far: dozens sent, zero paid yet). If you run outreach, how do YOU decide whether it's working?

## Bots (approved design)

Approved approach — three bot shapes, and one rule over all of them: **Keith is the approver on everything outward-facing. If it speaks publicly under this brand, Keith read it and approved it first.** This mirrors our existing email pattern exactly: software drafts, a human approves, then — and only then — anything sends.

1. **Drafting bots.** Prepare posts and replies for the community channels — first drafts of weekly posts, reply suggestions for threads we're in, summaries of discussions worth answering. Nothing a drafting bot writes publishes on its own; it goes to Keith, he edits or approves, and only then does it go out.
2. **A disclosed helper bot — our own Discord server only.** Clearly labeled as a bot. It welcomes new members, answers FAQs from the path (hours, links, "where do I start"), and hands out the repo and path links when people ask. It operates only inside our own server, where everyone can see exactly what it is.
3. **Listening bots.** Keyword monitoring and alerts — F5Bot-style alerts for Reddit, keyword alerts in Discord. They notify Keith when a conversation worth joining appears, so he can join real discussions early, as himself, instead of broadcasting into the void. Listening bots never post; they tap a human on the shoulder.

### Hard exclusions

- **No autonomous posting.** No bot publishes, comments, or replies without Keith's explicit approval of that specific item.
- **No bots posing as humans.** Anything automated is labeled as automated. No fake personas, no sockpuppets, no "team member" accounts that are actually scripts.
- **No bot activity in other people's servers or communities** beyond disclosed, rules-respecting human participation. The helper bot lives in our server only; listening tools only alert.

### Sequencing

Bots activate **after** the brand accounts exist and our Discord server is live — account creation is Keith's step. Until then, this section is the approved blueprint, not a running system: posts are written and approved by hand, and F5Bot (free, no build required) covers the listening job from day one.

## Cadence (once live)

- **Weekly:** one process/numbers post (rotating through platforms), daily-ish genuine participation in 3–5 threads, review the listening alerts each morning.
- **Monthly:** compile the best tactics other owners shared (with credit) into the path as updates; report community numbers in the open — members, real conversations started, path checklists completed that we know of.

## What success looks like in 30 days

- Real conversations with at least a dozen owners about what's working for them.
- At least three tactics learned from the community folded back into the path.
- People arriving at the repo from conversations, not ads.
- Zero spam complaints, zero rule violations. Growth that costs us our welcome isn't growth.
