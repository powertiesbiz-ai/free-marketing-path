# Thesis and research — what this project is actually testing

This repo is not just a how-to. It is a working test of a claim, run in public, with the numbers published either way. This page states the claim, what would prove it, what would refute it, and the questions we are asking other owners to help answer.

## The thesis

**A complete marketing system for a small business no longer requires meaningful money or technical skill.**

The functions that used to be agency-priced — website, search presence, content, CRM, automation, outreach, measurement — now exist at $0 in free and near-free tools — $0–≈$36/month if you add the paid conveniences PowerTies uses (its all-in spend, ring leader included, is ≈$56/month; see the proof log). Anyone who tells you the tools are the hard part is selling tools.

The real constraint was never cash. It is the **operator**: someone to sequence the work, execute it, verify it, and keep the loops running week after week. Most owners don't fail at marketing because they picked the wrong app. They fail because nobody is running the system on a Tuesday.

That operator — we call the role the **ring leader** — can now be filled by an AI agent directed in plain language by a non-coding owner. The human is not removed. The human stays as **approver**: nothing outward-facing leaves without a person saying yes. What's new is that the coordination, execution, and verification in between no longer require the owner to be technical, or to hire someone who is.

Four sub-claims sit under the thesis:

- **(a) The cost collapse is real.** Every function in the path has a free or near-free version that is genuinely good enough — not a crippled trial. PowerTies' path tools run at about $36/month in paid conveniences by choice (about $56/month all-in once the ring leader's platform is counted — the full ledger is in the proof log), and $0 is honestly possible.
- **(b) System first, niche second.** Standard advice says niche down before you build anything. PowerTies built the system first, and the niche sharpened afterward — through participation in the communities where the niche talks. We claim that's the more reliable order: a working system aimed roughly beats a perfect niche with no machine behind it.
- **(c) Published honesty outperforms polished claims.** Real numbers — including zeros, bounces, and failures — build more trust and more distribution than success screenshots. The proof log is the test of this, not just a virtue signal.
- **(d) A system researched in public improves faster than one researched alone.** Practitioners reporting what actually worked in their trade and town is better data than expert theory — including ours.

## What would prove it — and what would refute it

**The proof log is the lab notebook.** Its funnel metrics — sent → delivered → replied → paid → revenue — were defined before the results came in, and its checkpoints are committed to in advance (the first: an honest 30-day verdict on outreach, October 31, 2026). A lab notebook you only fill in when the numbers are good isn't a lab notebook.

PowerTies itself is an **existence proof** — one business, one non-coding owner, one AI ring leader. That proves the system *can* run. It does not prove it runs for anyone else.

The stronger test is **replication**: other owners importing the workflows in this repo, walking the path, and reporting what happened — through [field reports](../templates/field-report.md), issues, or the community. Ten owners in ten trades reporting straight numbers, good or bad, is worth more than anything we can say about ourselves.

**Refutation is real, and it's part of the deal.** If verified execution produces no movement by the checkpoints, a claim has failed. If replications consistently stall at the same step, that step — or the transfer claim behind it — has failed. When that happens, the log says so, in the same place and the same type size as the wins. If we ever quietly stop publishing, you have your answer about which outcome we got.

## The research questions

These are the five things we genuinely don't know and are asking the field — owners actually doing this — to help answer:

1. **Which no-cost/low-cost tactics actually produce customers** — observed results, by trade and context, not opinions or best practices?
2. **Where does the path break for other non-technical owners** — which step stalls them, and why?
3. **What is the true time cost?** The path claims $0 cash. Hours — the owner's, and whoever approves and helps — are the unmeasured currency, here and everywhere else in this space.
4. **Does the ring-leader model transfer?** Can other non-coders direct an AI agent — a paid one, or a free open-source setup like OpenClaw — to run this system? Or is our setup a special case?
5. **Which barter and partnership arrangements actually hold up over time** — and what terms (scope, value parity, timeline, in writing) make them last?

Every [field report](../templates/field-report.md) answers a piece of one of these. That's why we ask for hours and cash and exact results, not testimonials.

## Known limits (read before citing us)

- **N = 1.** So far this is a single case study: one business, one owner, one market. Treat it as a worked example, not a law.
- **Self-selection bias.** Community answers come from owners willing to answer. People report what worked for them; the silent majority who tried the same thing and got nothing is undercounted — which is exactly why we say a flop report is as valuable as a win.
- **No control group.** Nothing here is a randomized trial. When something improves, we can't always separate the system's effect from the season, the market, or the owner's existing reputation.
- **The ground moves.** Free tiers get cut, platforms change their rules, algorithms shift. A step that costs $0 today may not next year. The path has to be re-verified, not believed.
- **The founding owner is not a neutral test subject.** He directs AI agents unusually well — clearly, quickly, and with fast approvals. Whether the ring-leader model survives a less fluent owner is Q4, and it is genuinely open.

We publish these limits in the same repo as the claims because naming them is what makes this research instead of promotion. If you cite this project, cite the limits too.
