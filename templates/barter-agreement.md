## If one side cannot deliver

If either side cannot deliver as agreed, that side will notify the other by [date/number of days after learning of the problem]. The parties will first try, in writing, to [substitute an equivalent deliverable / extend the deadline / reduce the other side's obligation].

If they cannot agree within [number] days, either side may end this agreement. Work already accepted will be valued at the amounts above, and any remaining imbalance will be settled by [method] within [number] days.

## Other terms

- Each side is responsible for its own taxes, records, tools, and expenses unless written here: [exceptions].
- Work product and usage rights: [who owns what, and what license or usage right transfers on completion].
- Confidential information: [state any limits, or write “None beyond ordinary business discretion”].
- Changes must be agreed in writing — email is enough if both sides confirm.
- Neither side may present this barter as an endorsement unless the other agrees in writing.

> **Tax note:** In the United States, barter income is generally taxable at fair-market value. Organized barter exchanges may report transactions on Form 1099-B, but a direct trade does not become tax-free merely because no cash changed hands. Keep records and ask a tax professional about your situation.
